# Add and manage tasks

## Add a task

- **On a board:** **+ Add task** appears above each task list on a board 600px wide or narrower and in Obsidian mobile, and at the bottom of each cell otherwise. The green checkmark above the text box (or Enter) adds the task and keeps the box open for the next one; the X cancels. Cmd/Ctrl+Enter or **Details…** opens the Tasks editor first; Esc closes it. New tasks appear at the top of the cell. The task gets the cell's column and the date a drop into that row would set, unless you type a 📅 date yourself. Done has no add button.
- **Subtasks:** the list-plus icon on an open card (or, on hover, on a subtask) opens the same box under its subtasks. The subtask goes in the parent's note, after its last subtask, indented the way the editor indents (Settings → Editor → Indent using tabs), with no date or column of its own.
- **Anywhere:** the **Add task** command opens the Tasks editor and files the task with no column, so it shows in each matching board's first column.
- **Where they go:** by default, the end of today's daily note. Change this under **Settings → Task Trellis → New tasks**; existing users keep their saved heading preference. The note comes from the core Daily notes plugin's own find-or-create, so it has your usual name, folder and template, and Templater runs on it as it always does. A board can send its tasks to a specific note and heading instead. If the board's filters would hide tasks from the destination note, you get a notice.
- New tasks get a `➕` created date when the Tasks plugin's "set created date" setting is on.

## Work with cards

- Each board places a task with one tag, `#tt/<board>/<column>/<n>`, at the end of the line. `<n>` orders cards within a cell. A task can sit in different columns on different boards.
- Dragging a card to another row sets its due date: Due now → the last day of the window; a week row → the first day of that week (or the day after the window for a partial first week); Later → the first day after the last week row; No due date → removes it.
- The arrow at a card's bottom right is a shortcut for the most common drag: a Due now card moves to the next row, and a card in any other row moves into Due now. It keeps its column and goes to the end of the cell. Completed cards don't have one.
- With a No due date column (`undated-column`), undated tasks live in that column instead of a row. Dropping a card there removes its due date, and its tag keeps the column it had before, which is where its arrow sends it; dragging one out gives it the date of the row you drop it in. Completed undated tasks show in Done, in Due now.
- Dragging into the Done column completes the task, and dragging out reopens it. Checked tasks always show in Done. A recurring task's next occurrence keeps the column it was in.
- Clicking a card opens the Tasks plugin's edit modal. Without the Tasks plugin it opens the source line.
- The note name on a card opens Obsidian's editor over the board, at the task's line. Edit with your usual hotkeys; Escape or clicking outside saves and closes. **Expand** saves and opens the note in a tab, reusing one already open. Links in card text work the same way; Cmd/Ctrl-click opens a new tab instead. If saving fails, your draft stays open for retry, even when disabling the plugin.
- To open notes in a tab instead, set the board's **Settings → Appearance → Open notes and links in** to **A tab**. The note name then jumps to the task's line, reusing a tab that already shows the note in any window, and links in card text open in a new tab.
- Cards use theme-aware surfaces, line icons and a single metadata footer. Overdue cards have a slim red edge and a relative overdue label. A partial first week displays its actual date range.
- Tasks indented under a task are its subtasks. They show inside the parent's card, in note order, and go wherever the parent goes, whatever their own date or column. Completed ones stay visible, struck through, unless **Hide completed subtasks** is enabled in **Settings → Appearance**. That hides them on the whole board while keeping the counts, but a completed subtask with open subtasks of its own stays so they don't disappear. That settings section also includes **Sort completed subtasks to end of list** and **Add red bar to overdue tasks**. A chevron and completion count (for example **1/3**) above each list shares a row with the add-subtask button. Its tooltip says **Hide subtasks** or **Show subtasks**. The board remembers each list’s visibility. The **Subtasks** icon beside **Compact** opens a menu with **Collapse all subtasks** and **Expand all subtasks**. These apply to the whole board, including nested lists and hidden columns or rows, and are remembered when you reopen it. If the parent isn't on the board (filtered out, or completed long ago), its open subtasks show as cards of their own. To make a subtask a card of its own, drag it out of its card (on a touch screen, use its **⋯** button): above or below its card, or into any other column or row. Its line moves out of the parent's list in the note, with its own subtasks, to just after the parent, indented like the parent. A nested subtask becomes a card too, not a subtask of its grandparent. A completed subtask dropped outside Done reopens. The note changes for every board that shows it.
- **Compact** reduces card spacing without changing which subtask lists are expanded. Each board remembers **Compact**. Use the column picker to focus on one workflow column, especially in a narrow pane; that choice lasts for the open board view. Quick filter buttons such as **High priority** narrow the board while they're pressed and stay on until you turn them off; see [Quick filters](filters.md#quick-filters).
- On a board 600px wide or narrower (a phone, or a narrow pane on desktop), each column fills the board. A sideways swipe, a sideways trackpad swipe or the arrows beside the dots above the board moves one column; the dots show which column is in view. Column headers and row labels stay in view on every column.
- Open cards and subtasks have a ⋯ button; with a mouse, a subtask’s buttons appear when you point at it. **Move to…** lists the board's columns (in the card's row) and rows (in its column), with what the move also does, such as the due date it sets. The card goes to the end of the chosen cell, as if dragged there. A subtask's menu starts with **Below “‹card›”**, which makes it a card just below its card; its other choices make it a card in that cell. On touch screens the card buttons are 44px tap targets, and the add buttons show without hovering.
- On touch screens, drag anywhere along the handle on the right edge of a card to reorder it or move it into another date row. Hold near the top or bottom of the list to scroll while dragging. In the one-column view, drag sideways toward the left or right edge and hold for half a second to reveal the adjacent column. Move back into the list and release to place the card; continuing to hold at the edge advances through further columns. Swipe elsewhere on a card to scroll the list; in the narrow view, a deliberate sideways swipe moves between columns.
- After moving, completing or changing labels on a task, **Undo** restores the latest change, including a recurring task's generated occurrence. To protect subsequent edits, Undo skips any note whose content has changed since that action.

For color coding, see [Use colored labels](labels.md). For quick capture on an iPhone, see [Set up the iOS shortcut](ios-shortcuts.md).

Task Trellis’s label, board settings, new board, and fallback new-task editors use a green checkmark on the left to save or create, and an X on the right to cancel. These actions stay above the scrolling form on phones. The separate Tasks plugin supplies its own task editor and controls.

## Keyboard access

Tab to a card or subtask and press **Enter** to edit it (or open its source without the Tasks plugin). Use **Alt+Up** / **Alt+Down** to move an open card within its current column and date row. The card’s **⋯** menu provides moves to other columns and date rows without dragging. Done cards keep their completion-date order.

Tab through checkboxes, label controls and action buttons to use them directly. Settings tabs also support arrow keys, Home and End. Save with the green checkmark; Cancel or Escape discards an unsaved editor draft. Label assignment checkboxes save immediately; editing a label’s name or color requires the checkmark.


## Bulk triage

Click **Select** in the board toolbar, then select individual cards. Selection controls are separate from task completion. On desktop, **Command-click** (Mac) or **Ctrl-click** (Windows/Linux) on a card enters selection mode and toggles that card. **Shift-click** selects a range of displayed cards in the same column, starting from the last individually selected card; without an anchor it selects just the clicked card. Command/Ctrl-clicking a link keeps its normal link behavior. Normal editing and dragging return when you click empty board space or outside the board, choose **Done selecting**, or press Escape. Selection stays active while a bulk edit dialog is open.

Apply quick filters first, then use **Select all N** to select all matching main tasks, including tasks in collapsed rows and behind a “more” limit. The column picker limits this scope; **Select column** adds that column’s cards to the selection. Swiping between columns does not change your selection. Subtasks inside a card can’t be selected on their own. A subtask shown as a card of its own can, though label changes leave it unchanged, because subtasks don’t have labels.

Use **Move**, **Due date**, **Labels**, or **Priority** above the task list. Review the choices and save with the green check mark at the top of the dialog. Labels can be added or removed without replacing other labels. Moving to Done completes tasks; moving out reopens them. Moving into the No due date column removes dates; undated tasks moving out receive the end date of Due now. Other column moves preserve dates. Child tasks keep their own text and travel with their parent as usual.

Your selected tasks stay selected after a change, even if they no longer match the filter, so you can apply another action to the same group. Changing filters or the column picker clears the selection. **Clear** clears it manually. Each action offers one **Undo** for the successful writes; Undo also clears the selection.

Tasks that changed since selection or cannot be saved are skipped and reported. Successful changes in other notes are retained. Undo skips notes changed since the action rather than overwriting newer edits or sync changes. Clear and reselect skipped tasks before retrying.
